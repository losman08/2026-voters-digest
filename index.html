How to use this guide

A quick tour of each part — close with the ✕ when you're done.

📍

Choose your state

Tap the flag and state name at the top-left. You'll see which states are covered, switch between them, or request one that isn't listed yet.

⏳

The countdown

The colored bar shows how many days remain until Election Day, November 3. It turns amber in the final week and red on Election Day.

📋

Measures

Each card explains one ballot measure in plain English: what it does, what Yes and No mean, the cost, who backs and opposes it, and official links — including the full legal text as written by lawmakers.

🎯

Show the impact for you

On the Measures tab, pick one or more situations — renter, homeowner, senior, and so on. Each measure then shows how it could affect people like you. Use the topic chips or search to narrow the list.

👤

Candidates

Candidates shown side by side: background, where they stand issue by issue, endorsements, and a “Record & controversies” section that labels each item by how well it's verified. No scandal is invented.

🗳️

Before you vote

Official links to find your polling place and ballot drop boxes, track your mail ballot, and register — all from the Secretary of State.

✉️

Request a state

Don't see your state? Open the state menu at the top-left and tap “Request your state.” Your details stay private, and you'll be contacted when it's ready.

A note before you begin

Dear Family and Friends,

Every election, I watch people I love want to vote well and feel buried — ballots written in legalese, a dozen tabs open, arguments flying from all sides, and not enough plain truth. I built this for you.

This is a non-partisan guide. It gathers the official facts about your ballot — what each measure and candidate actually does, what a Yes or No really means, who stands on each side, and how it could touch your life — and puts them in one clear place, in plain language, with every claim linked back to its official source. It will never tell you how to vote. That part is yours, and it always should be.

Pick your state at the top, take your time, and vote with confidence.
